How to use the time series player

1. Data: a recorded motion

Paste rows of t, x, y into the box, or use Open file… for a .csv or .txt file.

  • t is time in seconds and must increase. It doesn't need to start at 0 or be evenly spaced.
  • x and y are table displacements in the units you pick (mm, cm or m). With only two columns (t, x), Y stays at 0.
  • Columns can be separated by commas, semicolons, tabs or spaces. Lines starting with # and a header line are skipped.
  • Values between samples are filled in by straight-line interpolation.

To play a real earthquake record, take its displacement time history, not acceleration. Pick its units, then use Amplitude % to scale it into the ±10 mm the table can reach.

2. Functions: a formula

Write x(t) and y(t) in mm, with t in seconds, and set a duration. Examples:

5*sin(2*pi*1.5*t)               1.5 Hz sine, ±5 mm
4*cos(2*pi*0.5*t)               pair with a sin() on y for a circle
6*exp(-t/2)*sin(2*pi*2*t)       decaying 2 Hz shake
5*noise(4*t)*min(t/2, 1)        random shaking that ramps up over 2 s

Available: + - * / ^ ( ), pi, E, sin cos tan asin acos atan atan2 sinh cosh tanh exp log log10 sqrt abs sign floor ceil round min max pow hypot, plus:

  • noise(u): smooth random wobble between −1 and 1. Larger multipliers on t shake faster. It gives the same result every time.
  • step(u): 0 before u = 0, then 1. For example, step(t-2) switches on at 2 s.
  • clamp(v, a, b): limits v to the range a to b.

3. Check, then play

  • The plot shows x (blue) and y (purple) against time, and the dashed line on the position pad shows the path.
  • Amplitude % scales the motion. Speed % plays it faster or slower: 200% halves the duration and doubles every frequency.
  • Anything beyond ±10 mm is cut off at the edge, and a warning says how much.
  • A warning also appears if the servos would need to turn faster than about 500°/s, since they can't keep up beyond that.
  • Play first glides the table to the starting point, then plays in real time. Stop, or reaching the end, glides it back to centre.
  • Update rate: 50 Hz sends a new target every 20 ms and handles motion up to about 10 Hz. 25 Hz is gentler on slow computers.
  • Keep this tab visible while playing. Browsers slow down hidden tabs, which makes playback stutter.

Earthquake (back-and-forth)

X axis · servo 1 · pin 9
Y axis · servo 2 · pin 10

The board sweeps each axis back and forth between its min and max at a constant angular speed. Settings apply live. Stop glides both axes to the middle of their range.

Assumes each arm tip pushes the table along its own axis through a long link or slot, so only its sideways travel moves the table and the axes don't affect each other. The servos can turn 270°, but they're limited to 45°–225° (135° ± 90°). At 45° and 225° the arm is fully out to one side; any further and it would swing back, so the table would reverse while the servo kept turning the same way. Gain is how far the table moves per degree. Tick Flip if the table moves the opposite way to the pad.
